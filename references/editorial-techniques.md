# Editorial technique index

Use this optional reference after [selecting the angle](editorial-judgment.md). Entries describe editorial mechanisms and their risks, not experimentally established benefits for scientific talks. The [source notes](editorial-source-notes.md) identify the evidence and limits. Several mechanisms can coexist, but naming more devices does not improve a title.

## Meaning and framing

| Technique | Mechanism and suitable information | Reader assumption and failure test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Contrast / antithesis | Aligns opposed outcomes or choices so the decisive difference becomes visible. Suits controlled comparisons and trade-offs. | Cases must be comparable. Check units, population, timescale and conditions; reject false binaries. |
| Paradox / expectation reversal | Creates tension between an established expectation and an observation; the explanation becomes worth hearing. Suits boundary effects and competing mechanisms. | Establish the expectation and resolve the apparent contradiction. An unfamiliar fact is not automatically a paradox. |
| Unexpected comparison / ranking | Gives scale or reveals an otherwise hidden ordering. Suits a defensible baseline, normalization or magnitude. | Name the eligible set and metric. Check absolute versus relative change and uncertainty; do not imply an omitted population was ranked. |
| Metaphor | Maps a familiar relationship onto an unfamiliar one, reducing the first explanatory step. | The audience must recover the same mapped relationship. State its limit when material; reject implied agency, intention or a false physical mechanism. |
| Double meaning / pun | One expression connects two relevant readings, adding a second interpretation to an intelligible message. | Both readings should help. Reject obscure vocabulary, culturally exclusive references, trivialized harm or a device needing explanation. |
| Irony / understatement | Invites the audience to infer a discrepancy between wording and established reality, or an unexpectedly large implication from restrained language. | Requires shared context and reliable tone. Avoid in risk communication, translated uncertainty, and any case where literal reading would mislead. |
| Framing / information emphasis | Selects the denominator, actor, consequence or reference point that makes a fact intelligible without changing it. | Check the complementary frame: relative versus absolute risk, average versus distribution, benefit versus cost. Do not hide a competing interpretation. |

## Syntax and sound

| Technique | Mechanism and suitable information | Reader assumption and failure test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Short declaration / active verb | Makes an established relationship easy to identify. Often suits a result or method purpose. | A concrete actor helps, but active syntax must not upgrade association to causation or personify a model. |
| Genuine question | Names a meaningful unknown and gives the next step a purpose. Suits introductions and unresolved conclusions. | Test presuppositions before adding a question mark; identify where the talk answers or bounds it. |
| Parallel structures / balanced clauses | Repeated grammar aligns cases and makes their relationship easy to compare aloud. | Parallel form can imply equivalent evidence. Preserve asymmetries and qualifiers even when they break the balance. |
| Fronting / information focus | Begins with the known object or necessary condition and emphasizes the new distinction. Suits neighbor continuity and boundary-dependent findings. | The reader needs a stable referent. Do not bury a decisive condition for a better final word. |
| Ellipsis / concise noun phrase | Omits recoverable syntax to speed identification. Suits dividers, definitions and chart topics. | Omitted relation must be recoverable from visible context. A noun phrase can label a subject without identifying a finding. |
| Two-part headline / subtitle | Splits the memorable idea from a concrete explanation, scope or comparator. Suits an unfamiliar topic or compressed finding. | Both parts must be readable as a unit. Do not use fine print to retract a stronger headline. |
| Alliteration / consonance | Repeats initial sounds / consonant sounds to connect already meaningful words. | Judge pronunciation, not spelling. Added sound should not force imprecise synonyms or distract from a serious finding. |
| Assonance / rhyme | Repeats vowel sounds / matching endings to make a phrase feel patterned. | An attractive sound is not evidence. Reject strained vocabulary and any extra conviction unsupported by the study. |
| Rhythm / stress / phonological wordplay | Groups spoken beats or exploits sound similarity to give related ideas a recognizable cadence. | Read aloud in the delivery language. Acronyms, numbers and technical names can disrupt cadence; preserve them rather than sacrificing meaning. |

## Curiosity, inference and the promise

Curiosity works when listeners know enough to recognize a meaningful missing relationship. State the object and stakes; leave the relationship to be explained, rather than withholding the basic subject. A useful question is specific enough that its answer changes understanding. An unspecified revelation or a dramatic tease with no scientific payoff is clickbait.

Inspect presupposition and implicature separately. A question about why a treatment works presupposes efficacy; a phrase such as *the cure gets cheaper* implies an established cure. A claim that a model *beats experts* may imply broad real-world superiority even when every stated benchmark number is correct. Test the natural inference against the actual task, population and comparator. Sound and grammatical fluency can make unsupported implications easier to accept.

## Domain boundaries that change the decision

Load the applicable row when the selected angle depends on these distinctions. This is one editorial framework; the scientific inference determines which safeguard matters.

| Domain | Distinction to preserve | How a shared device can fail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Climate, environment, energy | Scenario, region, time horizon, system boundary, adaptation assumptions, operational versus lifecycle effects. | A trade-off between modeled replacement mixes becomes an unestablished whole-grid emissions claim or policy ranking. |
| Medicine, public health | Population, design, endpoint, absolute and relative risk, follow-up, adverse events and uncertainty. | A striking relative reduction conceals a small absolute difference; an observational association becomes treatment advice. |
| Physics, chemistry, materials | Tested regime, scale, mechanism, control, measurement uncertainty and performance conditions. | An unexpected property at cryogenic temperature becomes a room-temperature breakthrough or a deployable device. |
| Biology, ecology | Species, setting, timescale, feedback, adaptation and tested mechanisms. | A metaphor gives organisms intention; one laboratory response becomes ecosystem resilience or evolutionary adaptation. |
| AI, computing, engineering | Dataset, task, comparator, hardware, compute or energy boundary, leakage and out-of-distribution behavior. | A benchmark winner becomes generally superior, deployable or more efficient despite unmatched resources. |
| Economics, social sciences | Identification, counterfactual, incentives, time period, distribution and aggregate versus subgroup effects. | An average improvement hides a disadvantaged subgroup; a correlation becomes a policy effect or welfare claim. |

## Transfer the function across languages

Preserve the scientific proposition, emphasis and promise; compose the wording anew in the requested slide language. English sound patterns, omitted articles and noun stacking do not transfer automatically. Chinese can use concise balanced clauses where the logical relation stays explicit. Japanese needs natural information order and modality rather than a translated English pun. Korean parallelism must preserve particles, endings and scientific register. These are checks, not language-wide formulas.

Read the complete target-language sequence aloud and inspect terminology, tone, causality and certainty against the source. Avoid an English cultural allusion unless this audience can recover it immediately. Prefer a natural direct statement when an equivalent rhetorical effect would strain the language. Follow the existing [translation preflight](scientific-fidelity.md#scientific-translation) for material ambiguities; a wordplay difficulty alone does not require asking the researcher.
