# Presentation brief

Use this reference to interpret the brief; use [guided intake](guided-intake.md) to collect missing answers and manage the host's question tools. The brief supplies a reason for the design choices that follow, rather than selecting a fixed template.

The brief establishes what the audience needs to understand and what the presentation must help them do with the research. Use the current request, the supplied slides, text or paper and any named [context profile](presentation-contexts.md) before asking for more information. A short request to create or refine slides is enough to begin; ask only for missing context that could change a consequential design choice.

Audience familiarity determines where explanation is needed. Listeners who know the field may still need an unfamiliar mechanism explained, while a research discussion may require methods and uncertainty to remain visible for scrutiny. Purpose determines which contribution, finding or decision should organize that explanation. When a narrower purpose has not been supplied, use the request to improve the research presentation rather than requiring an additional goal statement.

| Brief field | What to establish | Design consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Audience and familiarity | Who will listen and what relevant concepts they already know | Choose terminology, definitions and the conceptual support needed to follow the evidence. |
| Available time | The stated duration and any explicit Q&A allocation | Set the pace and decide how fully the explanation can develop during the talk. |
| Venue screen | Whether the venue screen is closer to 16:9 or 4:3, or an explicit answer that its shape is not known | Use the selected target canvas and reflow the layout. When the screen is unknown, retain an existing canvas; without one, disclose a provisional 16:9 design choice while leaving the venue ratio unconfirmed. |
| Technical depth | A concise overview, full technical detail or a decision delegated to palate | Decide which reasoning must be visible and which supporting material can be traced to notes or an appendix. |
| Purpose | The finding, mechanism, discussion or decision the deck should support | Establish the central argument and the evidence that needs the most attention. |

Time and depth work together. A short talk needs an economical route through its main evidence, while a longer one can develop more of the reasoning. Neither duration nor expertise determines a slide count or requires every derivation to appear. Honor supplied slide count, format and Q&A constraints, and establish whether listeners need to verify methods and results during the talk. A profile duration is a suggestion to confirm when time is missing; explicit current instructions override it.

Screen shape is a delivery constraint, so ask about it alongside duration and depth after establishing the setting and any external audience. Reuse an explicit 16:9 or 4:3 instruction rather than asking again. The source file's dimensions describe the existing deck, not the venue; when the screen is unknown, preserve those dimensions and record that the venue ratio remains unconfirmed. For text or a paper with no source canvas, use a disclosed provisional 16:9 design choice instead. Reuse an explicit delivery application or ask for PowerPoint or Keynote before native authoring; a document format does not imply that choice. A requested change of ratio requires a new output with a reflowed layout and a new symmetric work area, followed by native rendering and review. Stretching content to fill the canvas, cropping scientific evidence or reducing text below the minimum does not satisfy the brief.

Users may delegate technical depth to palate. Resolve that choice after inspecting the source and the brief, then record the decision in the transformation context. Keep units, labels, uncertainty, comparisons, limitations and sources wherever they are needed to interpret the evidence. Moving supplementary detail is appropriate only when its connection to the source and the visible argument remains clear.

Once the brief is resolved, carry these decisions into the [design system](design-system.md) and record them in the transformation context. Reuse supplied answers and leave ordinary visual decisions to the Skill. Single-page monthly updates are outside this multi-slide workflow.
