# Comparison compositions

A comparison should make the relevant differences inspectable without making unlike evidence appear equivalent. Choose the arrangement from the alternatives, criteria and evidence available in the source. These compositions are options, not a required template set. When none fits, design another arrangement that explains the actual comparison; do not force the research into two sides, a fixed item count or a scoring graphic.

Use the presentation brief and source material to make this choice without asking the user to select a layout for every page. Follow [scientific fidelity](scientific-fidelity.md) for evidence and [visual storytelling](visual-storytelling.md) for the explanatory contribution. Adapt colors, illustrations and wording to the target deck; reference placeholders and decorative assets are not research content.

## Choose the comparison structure

| Composition | Useful when | Check before choosing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Branched considerations | Two categories contain several short, source-supported considerations that should be read as groups. | The branches mean category membership, not causality or a one-to-one pairing between opposing items. |
| Shared-criterion pairs | Two alternatives have comparable measurements for the same criteria. | Values, denominators, units, scales and uncertainty support the comparison and any visual encoding. |
| Parallel qualitative fields | Two states, approaches or groups of considerations need a concise, substantive explanation. | Both sides have clear identities; aligned rows imply matched criteria only when the source supports them. |

### Branched considerations

Place two compact category anchors near the center and organize their considerations outward, with short headings, explanations and relevant visual cues. Simple branch lines can show which points belong to each category. The anchors establish the contrast; the surrounding content explains it. Keep enough wording to make each consideration meaningful rather than reducing it to a noun and an icon.

This can explain the supported advantages and limitations of one method, or two differently defined groups. Choose labels that match those roles. Two alternative methods are not automatically the positive and negative sides of a tradeoff. Branch counts may differ, and a point on the left need not have a corresponding point on the right. Do not invent extra considerations for symmetry, imply equal importance through an unexplained ranking, or turn membership lines into causal arrows.

### Shared-criterion pairs

Give each alternative a clear header, place the common criteria in aligned rows, and show the corresponding values on either side. This exposes a difference by criterion instead of making listeners compare two separate lists. Mirrored positions can work when the two sides have an unambiguous scale and direction. Explicitly label the alternatives and value meanings; a leftward mark must not be mistaken for a negative quantity.

Segmented indicators are one possible treatment for genuine quantities with a supported common scale. They are not generic ratings. Use them only when the block resolution represents the actual values faithfully. If fixed blocks would round away a meaningful difference, use a continuous chart, a dot comparison or a native table. Preserve exact displayed values, relevant units, uncertainty and missing-value meanings. Independent percentages must not be presented as complementary shares unless they actually sum to a common whole. Different units or definitions require clear row-specific context; do not invent normalized scores to make every row look comparable.

Criterion count follows the research. Neither the five-item ribbon limit nor the example's row count limits comparison rows. More alternatives, unmatched baselines or substantial uncertainty may call for a different composition rather than this two-sided form.

### Parallel qualitative fields

Use two coordinated content regions with distinct headers, consistent insets and readable text. A restrained fill or rule can identify each field. Pair a supported statement with its relevant depiction or evidence where useful; do not let a large decorative symbol displace the explanation. Opposing arrows or a central comparison badge are optional and should not suggest a process, conflict or winner that the source does not establish.

Align statements by shared criteria when they are genuinely paired. Otherwise, present each side as its own clearly grouped list without inventing row correspondence. Equal width does not require equal text length, equal numbers of points or equal evidential strength. This form can compare two approaches or organize benefits and constraints of one approach, but the labels must make that distinction explicit. Preserve conditions and caveats beside the affected statement.

## Adapt or design a different composition

Start with the scientific question the comparison must answer. Retain a verified chart when it already makes that difference clear. A native comparison matrix, aligned evidence panels, a small-multiple view or a source-supported before/after schematic may serve the material better than the patterns above. These are alternatives to evaluate, not more mandatory formats. A proposed before/after contrast must preserve what changed and what stayed fixed without implying an unverified treatment effect.

Keep source scopes, populations, time periods, baselines, definitions and evidence gaps explicit. Do not turn qualitative judgments into percentage bars, unknown values into zeros, or a partial comparison into an overall ranking. If the source does not establish a consequential comparison, retain that uncertainty and resolve the scientific question rather than decorating the gap. Record the selected composition and its reason privately with the content ledger. Creating a native table also follows the established [table-color choice](design-system.md#table-color-options).

Keep headers, explanations, criterion labels, framing and relationships native where feasible, with image-interior limits reported separately. Apply the symmetric work area, type hierarchy, top-anchored text and table-specific alignment. When a baseline, caption or other logical text remains on consecutive pages, follow [retained-text continuity](spacing-and-hierarchy.md#keep-retained-text-fixed-across-slide-changes). Reflow or split a dense comparison without dropping alternatives, criteria, qualifications or attribution.

Review content, editability and actual application renders separately. Check that the audience can identify what is compared, on which dimensions and under which conditions. Inspect labels, scales, uncertainties, connector meanings and every source-to-result association as well as alignment and visual balance. A balanced-looking pair of panels does not establish a fair scientific comparison, and a template match does not certify the final explanation.
