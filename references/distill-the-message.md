# Distill the message

Make each slide's selected message understandable without weakening the research. [Find the thread](find-the-thread.md) establishes why a slide belongs in the argument; this capability determines how its wording and visible detail fulfill that role. Simplicity comes from organizing necessary complexity, not removing it to achieve a minimal appearance.

## Edit in the service of the argument

Before rewriting, identify the slide's role, central message, supporting evidence and essential qualification from the private narrative map. A methodological slide may explain an operation rather than announce a finding. A question slide must not acquire an answer merely because a claim makes a stronger headline.

1. State what the audience should understand from this slide and what evidence or reasoning permits that understanding.
2. Organize that explanation into a readable hierarchy: title, necessary explanation, evidence labels and supporting detail. Keep the relationships among these elements explicit.
3. Remove repetition and wording that adds no meaning. Resolve overloaded scope through a coherent split or a narrower argument before shortening every statement.
4. Check wording against the source, including comparator, scope, units, uncertainty, assumptions and proposed versus completed status.
5. Return a changed title, claim or slide boundary to the [title sequence review](find-the-thread.md#slide-title-sequence-review). Local elegance does not justify breaking the argument.

Use connected sentences for continuous reasoning and structured lists or tables for genuinely separate items. Do not convert every explanation into fragments or force a fixed number of words, bullets or panels. A detailed method, comparison or result can remain substantial when its internal hierarchy makes the required reasoning clear.

## Retain sufficient visible explanation

Choose the necessary depth from the audience and time, then keep the method, evidence and reasoning needed for that selected argument on the main slides. Notes preserve supplementary material; retaining a full script there does not compensate for an underexplained presentation.

| Material | Usual location | Decision test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Central finding and evidence needed to assess it | Main slide or a clearly connected visible sequence | Can listeners understand both the claim and its basis during the talk? |
| Assumption, uncertainty, baseline or exception that changes interpretation | Beside the relevant explanation or evidence | Would moving it change what a reasonable listener concludes? If so, keep it visible. |
| Method operation, equation or definition needed for scrutiny | Main sequence at the selected depth | Can this audience follow or verify the inference without it? |
| Supplementary derivation, extended caption or secondary analysis | Mapped notes or appendix when not needed during the main argument | Can it be moved without hiding a limitation or removing the explanation's substance? |
| Material outside the agreed scope | Private source-to-output ledger, with the reason for omission | Is the selection truthful and complete for the claim being made? Do not omit contrary evidence merely to strengthen the story. |

For a short talk, select an intelligible research question and evidence path. For a longer research discussion, retain the assumptions, comparisons and unresolved issues that deserve inspection. Do not impose equal density across slides or presume that whitespace is evidence of clarity.

### Match visible detail to the explanation

Sherry's style uses visual structure to make substantial research content manageable. A slide can contain a composed illustration, several short method statements, a comparison and an interpretation when they serve one coherent question. The amount and kind of information in each region should follow its explanatory job. Equal panels do not require identical contents, and large empty areas are not a reason to remove information that makes a finding intelligible.

Before condensing a research explanation, identify what the audience needs to examine on the slide. Choose from the source material rather than completing a generic checklist with invented detail:

| Explanation | Content to keep visible when material to this talk |
| :--- | :--- |
| Method or model | Relevant inputs, key operation or relationship, outputs, study scope and the assumption needed to interpret that operation. |
| Experiment or comparison | What is held fixed, what varies, which cases are compared and how the comparison supports the stated inference. |
| Finding | Evidence, comparison baseline, direction and available magnitude, units, uncertainty and conditions that affect interpretation. A directional summary must not displace necessary source numbers or figures. |
| Contribution or implication | What was added or learned, the source-supported reason it matters and the scope or limitation of that implication. |

Keep these elements in a readable hierarchy: an entry statement, a useful diagram or evidence region, supporting explanation and a distinct interpretation where needed. Use aligned comparisons, nested groups, annotated diagrams or multiple coordinated panels before removing substance for a sparse layout. Preserve connected reasoning where short fragments would force the listener to reconstruct a missing step. A topic illustration and a broad conclusion alone do not establish sufficient detail.

For a short presentation, select a narrower coherent argument and explain its selected methods and findings adequately. Do not retain every section heading while reducing each section to a slogan. Track excluded branches and relocated material in the private ledger, and make a consequential scope change clear to the user. A supplied script may be retained in notes, but notes-only conservation does not prove that the visible explanation is complete. Move supporting derivations, secondary examples or the full original wording there only when the main narrative remains intelligible at the requested depth. Essential evidence and qualifications remain beside the claim.

## Make titles, labels and annotations do distinct work

Use the [headline method](find-the-thread.md#write-titles-for-their-narrative-role) to choose what a title says. Refine its language here without changing its scientific force. A title can state the main finding while a nearby annotation identifies the decisive comparison, magnitude or qualification; repeating the title beside the figure adds little. Preserve enough context that a direct label cannot be mistaken for another series, condition or panel.

Before adding a numerical insight, verify the underlying values, denominator, units, scenario and calculation. Distinguish a percentage change from a percentage-point change, an estimate from an observed value, and a contrast from a causal effect. Do not read precise values from a qualitative sketch or turn unresolved source discrepancies into confident prose. Scientific ambiguities become specific researcher queries.

Follow the detailed language and attribution rules below. [Scientific fidelity](scientific-fidelity.md) remains the authority for preservation, numeric display, unit labels and translation. [Writing style](writing-style.md) governs documentation and reports; README conventions do not prescribe slide phrasing or layout.

## Research slide wording

Begin a paragraph with its actual topic or message, then develop the evidence, explanation and qualifications the audience needs to follow it. Give each sentence a purpose within that explanation. Remove repetition and details that do not support interpretation or discussion, while retaining assumptions, uncertainty and other conditions that affect the finding. Shortening a sentence is useful when it makes the relationship easier to understand; fixed word limits and disconnected fragments are not substitutes for that judgment.

Avoid standalone disclaimer boxes that defensively list what a displayed metric or claim does not represent. State its scope directly in the quantity label, caption or relevant explanation; for example, name the metric "Power-sector investment" and make any material time basis explicit there. Remove redundant exclusions once that wording makes the scope clear. Keep interpretation-changing distinctions, including annual versus cumulative quantities and sector versus whole-system coverage, visible where needed under [sufficient visible explanation](#retain-sufficient-visible-explanation). Production notes, tool limitations and validation status belong in the delivery report or private review record, not audience-facing slide text; retain required attribution and confidentiality notices.

Describe what the evidence supports rather than announcing the presence of a figure. A source-backed finding or relationship usually gives the audience a more useful starting point than "Figure 2 shows...". Keep the source figure number and citation where they support identification and verification. Do not force a takeaway when the source supports only a question, method or observation, and never turn an association into a causal claim to make a heading sound decisive.

Define a research abbreviation at its first audience-visible occurrence, normally as the full term followed by the abbreviation in parentheses. Check the actual presentation order, including titles, tables, figures and build stages: a definition in later body text, speaker notes or a glossary does not explain an earlier visible abbreviation. Place the full term in the first title or an immediately associated visible explanation when that is where the abbreviation first appears. Use the abbreviation consistently within its scientific context; repeat its definition when a section or slide must stand alone or the audience needs a reminder. When distinct verified concepts share initials, make their full terms or local meaning explicit rather than merging the concepts or silently renaming protected identifiers. Avoid introducing an abbreviation that saves little repetition, and reduce unnecessary jargon according to the audience's familiarity.

Use definitions established by the source and its research context. If the full term cannot be established from the source and research context, or a material ambiguity remains, ask the user to clarify and wait for the answer before applying that expansion. Do not invent a scientific term from its initials. Symbols, chemical formulas, SI unit symbols, identifiers, codes and proper names need their appropriate explanation when unfamiliar, but are not automatically abbreviations to expand. Parentheses used to define an abbreviation remain valid; units attached to quantity labels use square brackets under the [unit-label rules](scientific-fidelity.md#unit-labels).

Review wording across the deck rather than checking body paragraphs alone:

| Check | Apply on slides |
| :--- | :--- |
| Spelling, grammar and research terms | Correct ordinary language errors, retain established scientific terms and use consistent spelling. Ask before resolving an ambiguity that could alter the research meaning. |
| Terminology and notation | Use one term for the same concept across titles, text, tables, axes, legends and notes. Preserve distinctions between different concepts; do not rotate technical synonyms for stylistic variety. |
| Quantity labels | Keep labels concise and specific, with the correct unit, symbol, case and exponent. Apply square brackets to unit-bearing labels without changing values or converting units. |
| Citations and cross-references | Keep each citation near the claim it supports. Prefer sentence-end placement when the association remains clear; keep a citation beside a particular clause when moving it would obscure that association. Preserve source figure, table and equation numbers when referring to those source objects. |
| Tone and emphasis | Report and explain the evidence with concrete nouns and precise verbs. Remove filler and unsupported praise without weakening scientific qualifications or suggesting greater certainty. |

These are slide-writing decisions. Manuscript requirements such as line numbering, fixed line spacing, submission filenames, mandatory acknowledgements or publishing data and code do not become presentation requirements. Likewise, do not number every new slide figure merely because a manuscript numbers its figures. Retain the source's references, attribution and required acknowledgements, and use figure numbers when the audience needs them to locate or discuss evidence. Rewriting follows the [content-changing workflow](scientific-fidelity.md#content-changingrebuilding-path), including a source-to-output meaning check; better prose alone does not establish scientific fidelity.

## Slide citations and references

Use APA seventh-edition author-date citation structure and bibliographic information. Place a concise source beside the evidence it supports and supply the corresponding full entry in the reference section. For three or more authors, use the first surname and `et al.` with the verified year, subject to APA disambiguation rules. Preserve organization authors, publication status and missing-date conventions where applicable; never infer a publication year or journal from a filename.

Set visible citation and reference text in italics as palate's slide treatment, with readable contrast and at least 10.5 pt. This full-line italic treatment is a house-style adaptation, not a claim that APA requires every word of a reference to be italicized. Use APA 7 for author order, date, title, publication details and DOI or URL, selecting the correct source type and using only verified metadata. The conventional APA emphasis still applies to bibliographic text supplied outside this slide treatment.

| Location | What to show |
| :--- | :--- |
| Claim or figure attribution | A concise italic author-year citation, adding an original figure or panel identifier when useful for discussion. Avoid long lists of PDF pages. |
| Reference section | Full, readable APA 7 entries corresponding to cited works. Retain publication page ranges or article numbers where bibliographically required; these differ from extraction-page locators. Split reference pages rather than shrinking type. |
| Private source ledger or speaker notes | Exact source pages, sections, figure identities, crop records and claim mappings used to verify the presentation. These do not become a routine visible footer. |
| Direct quotation or required credit | Preserve a necessary quotation locator and licensing or permission credit. The preference against routine page lists does not authorize removing required attribution. |

Keep citations attached to the claims they actually support, especially when one slide combines several studies. Do not label routine scholarly attribution as an AI audit, or use source labels as a substitute for explicitly explaining a material scientific uncertainty. A missing or contradictory source detail remains a verification issue; do not invent bibliographic metadata to complete a neat reference.

Consult the official APA guidance on the [author-date system](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/basic-principles/author-date), [paraphrases](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/paraphrasing) and [reference examples](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/examples) for source-specific cases.

## Review the edited explanation

Compare the visible slide and its connected sequence with the source, not just the new text with the old text. Confirm that terminology, equations, citations and qualifications remain associated with the correct claims. Pass the message hierarchy and required content to [Refine the visuals](refine-the-visuals.md); revise the scope or split when the composition cannot remain legible. After a visual change, recheck that the prominence and wording still imply the intended scientific meaning.
