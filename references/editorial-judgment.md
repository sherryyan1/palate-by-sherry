# Choose the angle before the words

This specialist guide serves agents choosing consequential titles within [Find the thread](find-the-thread.md#write-titles-for-their-narrative-role), and researchers reviewing those choices. Load it when an opening, finding or synthesis needs a stronger angle, or an attractive title risks changing the science. Routine corrections can use the canonical capability guide alone. BBC and The Economist provide observations to reason from; they do not define two output modes.

## Select a promise the slide can fulfill

1. **Establish the plain message.** Use the existing question/evidence links to state what is known, compared, modeled or still unresolved. Inspect the body and underlying evidence before looking for a memorable phrase. A title is a promise about what the audience will understand.
2. **Find the consequential angle.** Ask what this audience expects and which supported observation changes that expectation. Consider a comparison, mechanism, exception, trade-off, consequence or important uncertainty. Explain why it matters to the scientific question. A surprising ranking needs a defined eligible set, metric and time; a paradox needs apparently conflicting expectations under compatible conditions. Differences in denominators or populations can dissolve an apparent paradox.
3. **Match angle to narrative function.** Use the role table below. Decide what listeners must already know and what this slide prepares them to ask next. Familiarity and purpose govern the technique more strongly than the discipline or publisher.
4. **Choose the least demanding effective expression.** Keep the plain version unless a question, contrast or other device adds an identifiable explanatory or memorable relationship. Explore alternatives at important turns, without mandatory paired publisher candidates, quotas or another generation pass. Use the optional [technique index](editorial-techniques.md) when a device or domain boundary needs scrutiny.
5. **Test the promise and its implications.** Read literally, then ask what a reasonable listener would infer: causality, certainty, novelty, recommendation, universality or a winner? Verify those implications, not just the individual words. Keep an interpretation-changing condition in the title or immediately associated visible explanation. If essential support is absent, narrow the title or retain an open question; query the researcher only when the missing answer materially changes the work.

| Narrative job | Editorial choice and reason | Necessary restraint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Introduction | Open with an actual observation, consequential question or tension, then deepen it into a researchable gap. Each title should make the next scientific step worth following. | Define unfamiliar objects early; never invent a literature gap, conflicting evidence or mystery. |
| Methodology | Name the purpose, comparison or assumption that earns the inference. Precision → logic → clarity → elegance. | Keep exact method terms where needed; a pun must not displace inputs, operations or boundaries. |
| Results | Choose the finding that answers the question and the comparison that reveals its importance. Use a direct insight when supported. | A descriptive diagnostic or unresolved distribution can need a plain technical title. A point-estimate ordering alone does not establish a robust ranking. |
| Transition | Carry forward the established object and introduce the next distinction. | Avoid announcing an untested mechanism as the explanation for a preceding result. |
| Discussion or conclusion | State what the combined evidence changes in understanding, or the specific uncertainty that remains. | Distinguish inference from application; do not finish with an impact claim or slogan stronger than the results. |

## Keep the decision in the existing narrative map

For consequential titles, add the selected angle, audience assumption, editorial reason, implied claim, and promise/payoff location to the existing slide record alongside role, question/evidence IDs, neighbors and qualifications. A payoff may be the current body, a later finding, or an explicit explanation of what remains unanswered. These are judgments in the private map or `reviewer_notes`, not a new required extraction schema or a second ledger.

During the existing title-sequence review, read the Introduction alone, then the whole visible sequence. Check whether curiosity develops into a meaningful question, methods explain how it is tested, findings answer it and the ending synthesizes rather than repeats. Identify redundant openings, unanswered promises, premature answers and fatigue from repeated questions or jokes. Vary expression when it clarifies different jobs; do not vary accurate technical terms for decoration.

For a local wording adjustment under unchanged evidence and role, recheck the slide and its neighbors. A changed claim, comparison or question reopens its dependent evidence and payoff links. A move, split or merge reopens the affected sequence and lineage. Reuse valid analysis; complete the final sequence check after construction as already required.

## Borrow judgment with genre awareness

The [source notes](editorial-source-notes.md) separate observed wording and accessible methods from editorial interpretation. In the accessible BBC data cases, quantities and human consequences make relevance immediate. Selected BBC Future author-listed titles also use paradox, metaphor and cultural references. Economist data records combine analytical comparisons with qualifications; its public chart examples sometimes leave the finding to the figure beneath a playful heading. Its own writing guidance emphasizes purpose, audience and message.

These observations support choosing concrete relevance for an unfamiliar audience and analytical compression for a well-established comparison. They do not establish publisher-wide frequencies or exclusive techniques. Recoverability matters: wit that needs cultural decoding costs time in an international seminar. Transfer the explanatory relationship, then use [Distill the message](distill-the-message.md#make-titles-labels-and-annotations-do-distinct-work) for the actual wording and figure hierarchy. Preserve the existing scientific and native-application review requirements.
