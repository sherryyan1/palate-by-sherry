# Read the room

Understand what the audience needs before deciding how the research should be explained. A specialist may need an unfamiliar mechanism unpacked; an internal meeting may need unresolved assumptions visible for discussion. The setting is useful context, but it does not establish what every listener knows or prescribe a template.

This capability owns the communication brief and the reasoning behind depth, emphasis, tone and pace. [Guided intake](guided-intake.md) owns question order, answer handling and the existing helper schema. [Presentation contexts](presentation-contexts.md) supplies starting profiles. Use those contracts without adding new required fields for every possible presentation setting.

## Turn the brief into communication decisions

Read the request and source before asking questions. Reuse supplied answers and let users delegate ordinary design decisions. Begin with the guided setting, external audience when applicable, duration, venue screen and depth choices. Conference talks, defenses, interdisciplinary meetings and other custom settings can be described naturally; record their relevant details privately without forcing a new template or modifying the strict intake schema.

| Establish | Decision it informs | Boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Audience and relevant familiarity | Which concepts need explanation and what evidence listeners can scrutinize | A role or job title does not establish domain knowledge. Ask a targeted follow-up when that uncertainty would materially change the talk. |
| Speaker's purpose | What listeners should understand, remember, question or be able to decide | Infer a reasonable purpose from the request and source when clear. Do not demand a separate goal statement for an ordinary refinement request. |
| Available time and any supplied Q&A allocation | Scope, pace, explanatory depth and opportunities for discussion | Duration does not determine a fixed slide count. Do not invent a Q&A allocation. |
| Technical depth and verification needs | Which methods, assumptions, equations and comparisons remain visible | A short talk still needs interpretation-changing qualifications. Delegated depth is a decision to make after inspecting the material. |
| Occasion, tone and visual constraints | Formality, directness, conceptual support and suitable visual direction | An executive audience does not authorize an invented return on investment or policy recommendation. A public audience does not justify scientific oversimplification. |
| Venue screen and delivery application | Native canvas, reflow and usable output | The source deck's dimensions do not establish the venue's screen. Text or a paper does not imply PowerPoint rather than Keynote. |
| Presentation language | Wording, terminology and script-compatible typography | Keep it independent of README and conversation language. Preserve the source language unless a change is requested; resolve a materially unclear target. |

For an internal research discussion, prioritize the question, assumptions, alternatives and uncertainty that colleagues need to examine. For a tutorial, identify the learning objective and prerequisite concepts. For external specialists, establish the contribution with comparable evidence. For interdisciplinary or general audiences, explain the mechanism and significance before relying on unfamiliar terms. For decision makers, make the supported implications and decision limits legible. These are starting judgments to test against the actual audience, not fixed structures.

## Resolve constraints without expanding the interview

Use the [guided questions](guided-intake.md) to collect missing required context. Ask about familiarity, intended action or scope only when a plausible answer would change a consequential design decision. Ordinary font, layout and phrasing choices remain palate's responsibility. Required scientific or translation clarifications must receive an actual answer before dependent rewriting; independent source inspection can continue.

Offer 4:3, 16:9 and an explicit unsure choice for the venue screen. An unsure answer retains an existing deck's canvas. With no source canvas, use a disclosed provisional 16:9 design and leave the venue ratio unconfirmed. Match a chosen ratio through native authoring and reflow, then define the symmetric work area under [Refine the visuals](refine-the-visuals.md). Do not stretch content or crop evidence to fit. Reuse an explicit delivery choice; otherwise establish PowerPoint or Keynote before new-deck authoring. Follow [scientific translation](scientific-fidelity.md#scientific-translation) for requested language changes.

## Hand off a brief with reasons

Keep a concise private record of the resolved audience, purpose, time, depth, canvas, application and language, together with any material uncertainty. Add the communication consequences: what can be assumed, what must be explained, what deserves emphasis and what must remain available for inspection. Preserve explicit constraints and record the reason for a delegated choice. Do not add unsupported fields to the existing transform-plan schema; this broader reasoning belongs in the private authoring record.

Pass that brief to [Find the thread](find-the-thread.md). If the source cannot support the desired breadth within the time, choose a coherent scope rather than stripping every topic to a headline. Revisit the brief only when new source evidence or user direction changes the decision. Single-page monthly updates remain outside this multi-slide workflow.
