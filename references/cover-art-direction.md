# Cover art direction

A cover establishes the presentation's visual identity before the research explanation begins. Treat it as a complete composition: typography, image, color, scale and empty space should make one convincing opening. A relevant picture and a readable title are necessary ingredients in some covers, but their presence alone does not establish design quality. The finished slide should have the coherence of a carefully designed Keynote theme.

This is a separate design problem from the illustrated introduction. The introduction explains mechanisms and evidence in depth. The cover gives the subject a memorable visual presence and makes the title easy to enter. Do not shrink an introduction diagram onto the cover, add explanatory cards to fill space or inherit the body's title-and-content grid by default.

## Develop the composition before commissioning the image

Start with the exact title, the supplied author information, the research context and any mandatory institutional treatment. Identify which words carry the subject and which qualifications must remain visible. Then consider how the title itself can occupy the slide: its line breaks, width, weight and distance from the image establish the composition as much as the image does.

For a new cover or a substantial redesign, sketch at least two genuinely different compositions internally before choosing a direction. Change the spatial idea, not just the color or picture. For example, compare a title suspended in an atmospheric field with a typographic composition, or an organic illustration that grows across the lower edge with an immersive photographic crop. An approved cover needing a small correction does not need this exploration again. When the user asks to compare styles, produce rendered alternatives with the same title and metadata so the design differences can be judged fairly.

Write a short private art-direction brief before sourcing or generating a visual. Describe the composition, the title's position and scale, the shape of the visible subject, the area that must remain quiet, the palette and the intended medium. Include the scientific role and asset provenance separately. Do not commission an isolated square picture and only then decide where it fits.

## Choose a family, then design the particular cover

The following are starting points, not fixed templates or assignments by discipline. Select a family because its structure suits the title, the research identity and the occasion. Families can be combined when the resulting hierarchy remains clear.

| Family | Spatial idea | What makes the treatment work | Common failure to correct |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Typographic composition** | Let the title occupy the main visual field. Use a deliberate scale contrast, line break or offset, with restrained supporting color. | The wording has a clear reading order and enough space to feel composed. A long title can distinguish its subject from its scope without dropping either. | A standard title box stranded on an otherwise empty slide, oversized isolated buzzwords, or qualifications reduced to unreadable fine print. |
| **Immersive image** | Use a photograph or expressive field across the canvas, positioning the title in an area that is naturally quiet. | Crop, light and type belong together. A particular horizon, texture or architectural scale gives the subject a presence. | A stock picture in a rectangular panel, text competing with busy detail, or a generic dark overlay used to repair an unsuitable image. |
| **Organic editorial illustration** | Build around an open silhouette, a curved landform, an asymmetric cluster or an illustration that extends through the composition. | Flat color, controlled texture and a limited palette give the artwork a consistent hand. Its outline and internal negative spaces participate in the layout. | A boxed isometric city, a sticker pasted beside the title, swollen 3D objects, decorative icon clusters or an arbitrary blob mask around the same rectangular picture. |
| **Chromatic or material field** | Compose with a broad gradient, light transition, paper surface or large abstract form. The field establishes atmosphere while type supplies the research identity. | The color movement has direction, depth and restraint. Its calm region is planned for the title. | Generic neon swirls, glass bubbles, excessive glow, random gradients or an abstract shape that resembles an unsupported result plot. |
| **Object and scale** | Give a relevant object, specimen or material a striking crop or unusually generous scale within a quiet field. | The visible subject has character, a useful silhouette and a deliberate relationship with the title. | A small image inside a large empty frame, an unverified scientific object or a routine half-text/half-picture split. |

A title beside an illustration can still work when the illustration's silhouette, scale and visual language make that relationship compelling. It is not the default solution for every research topic. Likewise, a rectangular photograph can work as a purposeful full-canvas or edge crop; the problem is the unexplained picture box, not the geometry of every source file.

## Give the visual a specific language

Describe the medium through visible properties. For an editorial illustration, specify the shape vocabulary, edge treatment, texture, density of detail and palette. A flat cut-paper landscape, a restrained ink drawing and an atmospheric photographic field create different relationships with type. Choose one coherent treatment for each alternative. Instructions such as "premium," "Apple style" or "not AI-looking" are insufficient without these decisions.

Inspect the actual artwork before placing it. Reject generic glossy isometric scenes, indiscriminate blue glow, incoherent perspective, repeated synthetic detail and decorative infrastructure that the composition does not need. A few well-observed forms usually give an illustration more character than a catalogue of research-related objects. Preserve enough detail to make the subject recognizable without turning the cover into a technical figure.

Judge the visible silhouette rather than the raster canvas. Transparent art should have intentional outer contours and open space; an image can have alpha and still read as a square block. Preserve its proportions. Crop or mask only when that choice improves the composition and retains the relevant features. If the art still feels pasted on, revisit its scale, shape or medium together with the typography instead of surrounding it with a panel. When using microscopy, maps or other scientific evidence, preserve the scale, color encoding and annotations needed for interpretation. Do not redraw or modify observed structures for a more attractive background.

## Compose typography and space as a system

Retain the established Fira Sans family unless the user or required brand specifies otherwise. Choose the width and weight for the cover itself: a regular or light face can suit an atmospheric cover, while a heavier or condensed face can support a typographic one. Do not substitute Apple system fonts merely to imitate Keynote. Keep the title, scope, author names and affiliation as native editable text. Preserve their wording and scientific meaning; do not invent a presenter, date, affiliation or slogan.

Set line breaks deliberately and inspect their resulting shapes. A long title can span the canvas or separate its principal phrase from its scope while retaining a natural reading order. Resolve crowding through composition and line breaks before reducing type. The title, supporting scope and metadata should have distinct roles, with meaningful space between them. Avoid evenly distributing every element down the slide or filling every open area. Keep the minimum type sizes in the [design system](design-system.md).

Define a symmetric work area for foreground text, logos and standalone artwork. Background color, photographic fields and intentionally cropped background art may extend to the canvas edge; this must be a designed background role, not an excuse for accidental overflow. Use optical alignment when uneven silhouettes make mechanically equal gaps look unequal. Keep author information readable but subordinate. Navigation is normally unnecessary on a cover, and a page number is optional. Apply required institutional marks through the intended native master or layout and review their contrast against the chosen background.

Maintain a connection to the deck's palette without forcing every cover into the same white-and-teal arrangement. A dark petrol field, warm paper ground or nearly monochrome crop can belong to the same presentation when the color relationships and subsequent slides are coherent. Preserve any colors that already encode scientific meaning.

## Keep provenance out of the composition without hiding uncertainty

Do not place "conceptual image," "conceptual illustration," "image placeholder," design-option names or production notes on the cover. Store the visual's origin, artistic role, prompts and limitations in private records and appropriate speaker notes. These records are not part of the cover's visual hierarchy.

This does not authorize disguising invented imagery as evidence. A representative city scene must not imply a measured system, an actual installed project or the study's exact geography. Prefer visibly interpretive art, a verified source image or typography when a realistic scene would mislead. If a meaningful scientific qualification must be visible, use a different visual or preserve that qualification in a purposeful subject caption; do not suppress it for appearance. Required visible license attribution and confidentiality marks remain binding. Choose a different licensed asset when a mandatory credit would conflict with the intended cover.

Use Apple and Slidesgo references to study composition, not as a library of freely reusable artwork. Record actual asset permission separately from the influence on the design. An external example is not evidence that a new cover has been tested in Keynote.

## Compare the rendered covers

Render every alternative and inspect it independently at presentation size, then compare the set as thumbnails. Fix the composition before delivery when any of the following checks fails. Passing an overflow check is not sufficient.

| Review | Evidence to inspect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate hierarchy** | The title is easy to enter, its scope remains readable, and metadata has a deliberate secondary position. |
| **Integrated composition** | The image's crop or silhouette, title shape and empty space reinforce one another. No unexplained rectangular image block or incidental caption breaks the composition. |
| **Craft** | Line breaks, optical alignment, contrast, texture, image resolution and distances between elements hold up at full size. No crowded author line, dangling title word or cheap-looking generated detail remains. |
| **Meaningful variety** | Requested alternatives differ in composition, image treatment or typographic structure. Recolors and picture swaps within the same layout do not establish variety. |
| **Research identity** | The subject is recognizable through the title and chosen treatment without invented results, infrastructure or scientific relationships. |
| **Native usability** | Title and metadata remain editable. The delivery application's render and an edit-save-reopen check confirm behavior; image-interior editability limits are recorded honestly. |

Record the choice and unresolved limitations privately. Keep PowerPoint and Keynote validation separate, following the [application workflow](scientific-fidelity.md). If native review is unavailable, state that limitation instead of claiming a verified application result.

## Composition references

These observations come from rendered theme previews rather than Apple's event campaigns. They inform the families above without becoming mandatory layouts or assets to copy.

| Reference | Observed composition | Design lesson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [Apple's Keynote theme chooser](https://support.apple.com/en-mide/guide/keynote/tan317e80e8c/mac) | Standard Minimalist uses a dark field and sweeping light form; Scientific Muted anchors the title low on an ochre field; Business Modern uses architectural photography. | Vary the whole spatial arrangement, including title position and image scale. |
| [Apple Keynote showcase](https://www.apple.com/sg/keynote/) | Dynamic themes use full-canvas color and light fields with quieter regions for text. | Design the background and typography together. |
| [Slidesgo: Nature Inspired Design](https://slidesgo.com/theme/nature-inspired-design) | A large leaf photograph and dark water form the field for a white title. | A purposeful close crop can express a subject without depicting every component. |
| [Slidesgo: Gorgeous Minimalist Slides](https://slidesgo.com/theme/gorgeous-minimalist-slides) | Centered serif type, a warm pale field and faint cropped foliage. | Let material and spacing support the title rather than fill the page. |
| [Slidesgo: Masterclass on Nature with Flat Style](https://slidesgo.com/theme/masterclass-on-nature-with-flat-style) | A mint field, irregular terrain and illustration entering through the lower and right edges. | Use silhouettes and edges to integrate illustration into the composition. |

These are visual observations and palate's design judgments, not rules or endorsements published by Apple or Slidesgo. Theme availability can change. Inspect the actual preview when using a reference for a new task.
