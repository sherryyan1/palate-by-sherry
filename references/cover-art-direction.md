# Cover art direction

A cover introduces the research before the explanation begins. Its visual focus should therefore come from the research question, scientific object or setting. Choose the subject first, then give it enough quiet space to establish a clear relationship with the title. An attractive background is useful when it supports that introduction; it cannot supply a connection to the research on its own.

This guide uses Apple presentation and event imagery as a composition reference. The observed examples vary: the [Keynote showcase](https://www.apple.com/sg/keynote/) pairs condensed typography with ocean photography, the [WWDC24 announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/03/apples-worldwide-developers-conference-returns-june-10-2024/) makes stacked lettering the central object, and the [WWDC25 announcement](https://www.apple.com/ca/newsroom/2025/05/apples-worldwide-developers-conference-kicks-off-june-9/) places a pale dimensional form above a separate event name. These examples establish a range of compositions rather than one Apple formula. The directions below adapt their use of focus, hierarchy and space to research communication; they are palate's design judgments, not Apple's research presentation rules.

## Choose a direction from the subject

Read the brief and source material before choosing the cover treatment. A concrete research object can give the title an immediate visual anchor; a systems question may benefit from a small, accurate schematic. When imagery would add little or misrepresent the subject, a carefully composed typography-led cover can be stronger. The directions overlap and do not assign a fixed template to each discipline.

| Direction | When it can help | Composition to develop | Scientific check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subject in focus** | An object, specimen, instrument, material or technology is central to the question. | Give one relevant subject a substantial visual presence, with an editable title beside or above it and an open surrounding field. Choose a light or dark ground according to the subject's visibility. | Verify the depicted features. A representative object must not imply that it is the actual specimen or apparatus used in the study. |
| **Place and scale** | The research depends on an environment, infrastructure, geographic context or field setting. | Use one purposeful photographic crop or contextual illustration. Place the title in genuine negative space or a separate quiet field, preserving the features that make the setting relevant. | Establish whether the scene is observed, representative or conceptual. Do not imply a study location, event or operating condition that the source does not establish. |
| **Mechanism as identity** | A network, interaction, process or system relationship defines the problem. | Build a restrained schematic around a few source-supported components and connections. Let the motif introduce the question without compressing the entire methods figure onto the cover. | Check each connection, direction and grouping. Decorative networks must not invent causal relationships, spatial organization or measured quantities. |
| **One idea, one form** | The contribution is abstract, or no trustworthy pictorial subject improves the introduction. | Use a deliberate type hierarchy with one restrained mathematical, geometric or evidence-derived motif. A result may serve as the focal form when its meaning remains legible with the context and qualification it needs. | Preserve symbols and distinctions. Do not use decorative equations, invented trajectories or an unlabelled data-like shape that could be mistaken for a finding. |

Record the selected direction and its reason in the private design specification. Explain what the focal element introduces, why its scale suits the title and how the quiet areas support reading. When the first proposal feels interchangeable with an unrelated deck, or two treatments have different advantages, develop a useful alternative internally and compare them. This is part of design judgment; it does not require another user interview unless a material subject, asset or branding decision remains unresolved.

Judge prominence from the visible subject and its relevant detail, rather than the area of its image frame. A tiny specimen image does not become a strong focal subject simply because it sits in a large panel. For a field photograph, inspect the actual crop before reserving title space so an overlay does not conceal the feature that connects the scene to the research. When the exact title is the only trustworthy material that improves the introduction, a deliberate typography-led composition is a valid outcome. Do not invent a motif merely to make it appear more distinctive.

## Compose the title, subject and space together

Keep the established Fira Sans title hierarchy and the deck's coherent teal identity or approved scientific palette. Apple-inspired composition does not require San Francisco fonts, Apple marks, device mockups or copied campaign artwork. Neon edges, glass effects and gradients belong only when they serve the particular subject and preserve scientific meaning; they are not a cover preset. The same principle applies to a black background: choose it for a visible design reason rather than as a substitute for art direction.

Set the title, subtitle and supplied presenter or date as native editable text. Give the title a clear entry, then separate supporting metadata through scale and spacing so the audience can distinguish their roles. Preserve the wording and scientific scope of the title. A long title may need thoughtful line breaks or a different composition; shrinking every element or hiding important qualifications does not resolve the hierarchy. Follow the [design system](design-system.md) for fonts, minimum sizes and stable text placement, and [spacing and hierarchy](spacing-and-hierarchy.md) for optical review.

Define the symmetric work area before arranging foreground elements. Keep the title, metadata, foreground object, diagram, labels and required attribution within it. A background photograph, texture or color field may fill the canvas; that background role does not permit foreground text or explanatory labels to spill beyond the work area. Crop a full-bleed image without losing the subject features that establish its meaning. The bottom-right slide number retains the work-area exception when the cover uses one. Navigation is optional on a cover and should appear only when it helps orientation. Apply required institutional marks through the appropriate native layout or master, respecting their established treatment.

## Keep imagery honest and usable

Establish the image's role before using it. An observed image shows a documented specimen, place or event; a representative image introduces a relevant class or setting; a conceptual illustration explains an idea. Keep that distinction clear wherever confusing it could change the audience's interpretation. Generated imagery must not masquerade as measured evidence, an observed site, a real specimen or a validated mechanism. Retain native editable labels and scientifically meaningful connections outside a bitmap asset where feasible.

Use assets with a recorded origin and appropriate reuse permission. Apple examples inform composition and do not become assets for research covers merely because they are publicly viewable. Check resolution at the final placed size, protect essential image features and review generated details against the source. A native geometric or schematic treatment may offer better accuracy and editability than a photographic approximation. Do not force a pictorial cover when no suitable visual contributes to the subject.

## Review at two scales

Inspect the cover at full slide size to check title fit, contrast, glyphs, spacing, image detail and metadata. Then inspect a thumbnail to judge whether the topic, main focal element and title hierarchy remain apparent. The quiet areas should support the composition, while the image and title should read as one introduction rather than unrelated objects sharing a canvas.

Complete scientific and editability review separately from that visual assessment. Check any evidence-derived motif against its source, retain its essential qualification and verify that title and metadata remain editable in the delivery application. A preview does not establish native PowerPoint or Keynote behavior. If the required application review cannot be completed, keep that validation pending and state the limit under the [scientific fidelity workflow](scientific-fidelity.md).
